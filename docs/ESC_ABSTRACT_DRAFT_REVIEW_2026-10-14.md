## Candidate abstract — working draft (English)
**Provisional title:** Endothelial response to flow after simulated microgravity: investigating the contribution of surface heparan sulfate

**Background.** Exposure to altered gravitational loading is associated with cardiovascular adaptation, but endothelial responses vary with the experimental model and hydrodynamic environment. The endothelial glycocalyx contributes to flow sensing, yet observations across distinct vascular beds and gravity analogues do not establish whether surface heparan sulfate mediates the response to a subsequent shear-stress challenge.

**Working hypothesis.** Prior exposure to simulated microgravity modifies endothelial responsiveness to a controlled flow challenge, and perturbation of surface heparan sulfate changes this response under otherwise comparable conditions.

**Main objective.** Evaluate the feasibility of a controlled experimental framework separating previous exposure, glycocalyx-related perturbation, and acute flow stimulation, while identifying a measurable endothelial signaling response.

**Strategy.** In an appropriately equipped host laboratory, an exploratory factorial design would compare reference-gravity and simulated-microgravity conditions, with a glycocalyx-related perturbation and flow versus no-flow stimulation. A short placement would first assess system readiness, cell integrity, assay suitability, and reproducibility of a predefined signaling readout. Direct nitric-oxide measurements, cross-donor replication, and mechanistic rescue studies would be reserved for a subsequent, adequately powered investigation.

**Expected results.** The pilot may establish whether the protocol and measurements are sufficiently robust to support a mechanistic study. Both a change and no change in the flow response would be informative, provided technical controls support interpretation. Alterations in eNOS-associated signals would not alone establish nitric-oxide production, specific glycocalyx mediation, cardiac effects, or clinical benefit.

**Scientific outlook.** This approach could clarify how gravitational loading history interacts with endothelial mechanobiology and define whether more integrated endothelial–cardiomyocyte models are warranted for subsequent research into myocardial adaptation and HFpEF.

## Scientific scrutiny before submission
- The current materials list work combining altered gravity and flow, and separately work connecting glycocalyx components to flow responses. **Novelty remains conditional on verified full-text methods**, model and timing.
- Simulated microgravity devices can introduce rotation-related flow and other confounding factors; attribute effects to the experimental condition until adequately separated.
- A signaling endpoint (e.g., phosphorylated eNOS) is not a validated direct readout of NO availability.
- A short training placement does not establish efficacy, causal mediation or clinical relevance to HFpEF.
- An intervention affecting HS may alter multiple molecular interactions. Interpret with suitable controls and orthogonal verification.
- The author has **not selected this hypothesis**; maintain alternative intrinsic cardiomyocyte and integrated eGCX–titina pathways as comparators.
- This text reports **no preliminary experimental results**. Do not insert fictional experiments or accomplishments.
